# Adaptive cascade: first controlled EXP holdout

Run date: 2026-08-30. The bilingual smoke completed 2/2 requests without
runtime errors. The controlled holdout then completed 10/10 requests without
errors: 4 were `accepted` and 6 were `best_effort`. OpenRouter cost was
$0.398374; Modal GPU/CPU cost is not included in the local usage record.

This is a useful algorithmic signal, not a production-readiness result. The
aggregate 40% acceptance rate hides a complete language split:

| Language | Requests | Accepted | Selected clears EXP | Quality-compatible EXP clear exists |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| English | 5 | 0 | 3 | 4 |
| Italian | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4 |
| Total | 10 | 4 | 8 | 8 |

All four accepted candidates came from the first conservative round at 35%
requested rewrite strength. They preserved the deterministic entity, number,
quotation, negation, and terminology contract; the frontier judge reported no
material errors and assigned fluency and naturalness 5/5. Their mean token edit
ratio was 0.2946, mean semantic similarity 0.9894, and mean bidirectional NLI
0.9987.

## Detector interpretation

The selected candidate cleared the compatible EXP detector in 8/10 cases.
Across the generic ensemble, selected-candidate clear counts were Binoculars
7/10, Fast-DetectGPT 9/10, LogRank 10/10, and RADAR 5/10.

RADAR explains the language split: it detected all five English sources and all
five selected English rewrites, while it detected none of the five Italian
sources or rewrites. No quality-passing English candidate cleared the complete
generic ensemble. This is evidence that the current OpenAI-only cross-family
rewrite does not remove the English statistical signal seen by RADAR; it also
means the Italian 4/5 acceptance rate must not be interpreted as equally strong
generic-detector evidence.

One Italian `best_effort` candidate cleared EXP and every generic detector but
was correctly rejected for an entity-integrity failure. The quality gate is
therefore actively discriminating rather than rubber-stamping rewrites.

## Boundaries

- The adaptive cascade had never seen these ten rows, but the rows came from
  the earlier fixed-grid Gate 2c confirmation corpus. This is an adaptive-method
  holdout, not a generation-level virgin corpus.
- Selection was conditional on pre-attack EXP detection: 25 English and 27
  Italian held-out rows were eligible, and five per language were selected by
  a seeded SHA-256 ordering.
- The sample is deliberately small. Four successes give no narrow confidence
  interval, and zero English successes is a blocking result.
- Two accepted candidates are close to at least one decision boundary. The
  weakest accepted EXP margin was -0.003 and the weakest accepted Binoculars
  margin was -0.011, so repeat/provider perturbation robustness is not yet
  established.
- All sources were generated by Qwen, so the Qwen rewrite route was excluded.
  The tested cascade used GPT-5.6 Terra as its only eligible rewrite family.
  Position-aware BIRA was not run because using the configured Qwen logit-bias
  route would violate the cross-family constraint.

## Decision

Keep the cascade as the candidate architecture. Do not yet label it reliable or
ship a binary “watermark removed” claim. The next tuning work should use only a
development split and add a second non-Qwen rewrite family, with the primary
objective of reducing English RADAR risk without weakening entity integrity.
After freezing that change, evaluate on a fresh confirmation corpus rather than
rerunning these ten rows as a new holdout.
